## Security assessment

### Manifest snapshot

GitHub's 2026-11-22 snapshot has **7 open alerts: 1 critical, 3 medium and 3 low**.
The already-merged #712 updates the Torch/Transformers set; the former 39-alert
baseline must not be copied forward as current. Advisory severities can change,
so a numerical decrease alone is not proof that every previous finding was fixed.

| Package | Severity | Advisory | Disposition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `python-jose` | critical | [GHSA-3qf3-8w2g-rqmx](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-3qf3-8w2g-rqmx) | No patched version reported in the snapshot. AVA decodes only HS256 with a configured symmetric secret; assess that bounded exposure and retain the alert. |
| `minimatch` | medium | [GHSA-g3hv-75wg-m249](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-g3hv-75wg-m249) | Frontend development dependency in the lockfile; fixed version reported as 11.0.1. Remediation remains separate dependency work. |
| `@vitest/mocker` | medium | [GHSA-qxg6-89mg-rqv8](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-qxg6-89mg-rqv8), [GHSA-xp26-3454-mjgp](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-xp26-3454-mjgp) | Frontend development tooling; dev-server mock registration can inject JavaScript, and one traversal affects Windows. Fixed version reported as 4.1.12; keep dev servers private. |
| `dompurify` | low | [GHSA-cg2g-xh64-v4q7](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-cg2g-xh64-v4q7), [GHSA-mrv2-3pqr-j5jr](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-mrv2-3pqr-j5jr), [GHSA-c6gr-qvv4-5v3f](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-c6gr-qvv4-5v3f) | Monaco dependency; fixed version reported as 3.4.17. Admin authentication in `admin_ui/backend/auth.py` fixes `ALGORITHM = "HS256"`
and passes `algorithms=[ALGORITHM]` to `jwt.decode` using the private configured
secret. That is evidence against this advisory's asymmetric-public-key-as-HMAC
path in AVA's configuration, not a repaired `python-jose` package or universal
consumer clearance. Keep management interfaces private and retain strong secrets.

### Image and Bookworm evidence

The earlier [main Trivy run](https://github.com/hkjarral/AVA-AI-Voice-Agent-for-Asterisk/actions/runs/44453991747)
on `da7dddbc` completed with 10 unique Engine and 14 unique CPU Local AI results,
including high packaging findings for `jaraco.context 5.3.0` (CVE-2026-23949) and
`wheel 0.45.1` (CVE-2026-24049). The Local AI count includes Dockerfile configuration
findings; counts are not distinct CVE totals. The workflow uses `ignore-unfixed`
and a CPU core profile, so green status is completion, not security clearance.
A [fresh release-branch scan](https://github.com/hkjarral/AVA-AI-Voice-Agent-for-Asterisk/actions/runs/45091779357)
on `d7bd7d46` completed with 10 Engine and **42 CPU Local AI results**. Engine
findings are unchanged. Local AI adds 28 package/advisory results across
`libc6` and `libc-bin` at `2.41-12+deb13u4`: **14 distinct advisories**, comprising
26 medium and 2 low results. Trivy reports `2.41-12+deb13u5` as fixed. These are
newly reported findings against the scanned package set; the release-doc changes
do not introduce a libc dependency change. No new critical/high image result was
reported, and the earlier packaging-tool highs remain.

The added IDs are CVE-2026-18374, CVE-2026-19499, CVE-2026-19542,
CVE-2026-5435, CVE-2026-6238, CVE-2026-6368, CVE-2026-6791,

CVE-2026-89092, CVE-2026-95818 and CVE-2026-97399. Reconcile the linked SARIF
when assessing remediation rather than interpreting 28 results as 28 CVEs.
The maintainer authorized the focused image fix in [#889](https://github.com/hkjarral/AVA-AI-Voice-Agent-for-Asterisk/pull/889),
now merged as `15cc1e7e`. Hosted logs on reviewed `8a6f253f` verify both packages
at `2.41-12+deb13u5` in builder and runtime. Its final ready-state scan and the
[exact-main scan](https://github.com/hkjarral/AVA-AI-Voice-Agent-for-Asterisk/actions/runs/45100653800)
remove all 28 targeted results, leaving **10 Engine /14 CPU Local AI results**
with no additions. The guard checks both packages independently and fails on
old/missing versions even in lean profiles. Fourteen synthetic guard cases and
hosted build/version assertions support that claim; no live deployment is inferred.

The two inherited packaging highs are setuptools-vendored copies in system and
venv paths: [jaraco.context path traversal](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-58pv-8j8x-9vj2)
can write outside a tar extraction directory, while [wheel unpack traversal](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-8rrh-rw8j-w5fx)
can change permissions outside the destination. No direct AVA handler invoking
those extraction entrypoints was found. Upgrading standalone packages alone
may leave the vendored copies present; remediation needs compatible packaging
changes or removal. Remaining pip/setuptools and Dockerfile configuration results
stay in the linked SARIF and are not dismissed.

Engine, Admin and updater retain Bookworm bases. On 2026-11-22 Debian's trackers
still list Bookworm zlib `1:1.2.13.dfsg-1` as vulnerable to
[CVE-2026-85091](https://security-tracker.debian.org/tracker/CVE-2026-85091)
and [CVE-2026-27171](https://security-tracker.debian.org/tracker/CVE-2026-27171);
the Trixie fixed version matches the CPU Local AI Dockerfile's enforced minimum.
Debian also lists Bookworm glibc as affected by the representative
[CVE-2026-18374](https://security-tracker.debian.org/tracker/CVE-2026-18374), with
the Trixie fix at `2.41-12+deb13u5`. Updating Local AI's Trixie packages would not
clear Bookworm exposure in other services.
This is current vendor/base-family evidence, not an independent inspection of
installed packages in every final candidate image. Recheck candidate artifacts
before publication; `ignore-unfixed` can hide these findings.

**Historical attribution correction:** the [v7.7.3 Bookworm assessment](v7.7.3-validation-matrix.md#bookworm-exposure)
also named CVE-2026-76844 as zlib. The [current Debian record](https://security-tracker.debian.org/tracker/CVE-2026-76844)
identifies webpack-dev-middleware instead. That ID is not carried forward as a
zlib finding. The historical record is preserved with this explicit correction;
no claim is made that another package's exposure was thereby fixed.

The local Docker daemon was unavailable during preparation, so no fresh local

### Proposed v7.7.4-only exception — approval pending

For maintainer review, propose accepting only the known remaining baseline for
v7.7.4: the seven manifest alerts above, the recorded 10 Engine /14 CPU Local AI
image results, and the listed Bookworm zlib/glibc exposure. This is a proposal,
not an approved exception or a claim that vulnerable packages are repaired.

- Keep Admin/Docker management restricted, with the fixed HS256 algorithm and a
  strong private symmetric secret. Do not expose frontend dev/test servers.
- Retain current Monaco sanitizer modes and use trusted model/package/archive
  inputs. No general XSS or arbitrary-model safety claim is made.
- Release maintainers own remediation review by **2026-11-29 or before the next
  stable tag, whichever comes first**, including compatible frontend and vendored
  packaging fixes plus Debian updates or separately validated base-image changes.
- Newly identified findings require separate assessment. The standard filtered
  CPU scan and reused runtime evidence do not qualify every optional/GPU profile.
  The old v7.7.3 exception/deadline is not extended retroactively; no alerts are
  dismissed. Merge and tag still require explicit approval.
